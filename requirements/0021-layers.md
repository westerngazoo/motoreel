# R-0021 — Layers and the peel («capas», «pelar»)

- **Status:** Accepted, In progress (implemented; PR open)
- **Milestone:** MC (the creator pipeline)
- **Owner:** Gustavo Delgadillo (westerngazoo)
- **Created:** 2026-11-22
- **Depends on:** R-0020 (meshes from OBJ, layer tags), R-0019 (`XRay`,
  `Sweep`), R-0018 (meshes, the object-level painter's order), R-0017
  (`Material`, `Layer`, `Faceted`)
- **Realized by:** [SPEC-0021](../specs/0021-layers.md)
- **QA:** `qa` agent run scoped to this requirement

## 1. Statement

motoreel must render a figure in **three nested layers** (skin, muscle,
bone) and take them away one at a time:

1. **The peel.** The skin opens in facets: each face lifts a little along
   its own outward normal and fades, eased, staggered across the figure,
   all a pure function of one parameter.
2. **The muscle.** Under the peeled skin the muscles show, each group its
   own material, and any named group can be lit in another colour, eased
   in and out.
3. **The bone.** The muscles turn to glass and the bones show, with the
   existing `XRay` finish and `Sweep` (R-0019).

The three layers ride the same motors, part by part, and the depth sort
draws a part inside another part first, at every depth of nesting.

## 2. Rationale

The owner approved a «toy anatomy» for the fbf reels on 2026-11-22: «eso
elevaría el juego a nivel pro». A lift is explained by what works under
the skin; today the x-ray jumps straight from skin to bone. Third-party
anatomy models were rejected (a paid écorché with a NoAI notice; a CC-BY
skeleton whose credit would ride every reel), so the layers are modelled
in-house with `tools/blender/` and loaded with R-0020.

Nesting is where R-0018's order is weakest: an off-centre muscle inside a
limb has no separating face plane with the limb's skin, and the mean-depth
fallback can put the muscle **over** the skin that hides it. That has to
be exact before a reel stacks three layers.

## 3. Acceptance criteria

- **AC1. Nested parts are drawn inside out.** When every vertex of one
  object lies on the inner side of every face plane of another (it is
  enclosed by the other's convex hull of planes), the enclosed object is
  drawn first, whatever the camera and the insertion order. This holds
  for three levels (bone in muscle in skin), for a part off-centre in its
  shell (where the mean-depth fallback fails, shown by a test), and for a
  peeled shell. When the pairwise orders of a depth group form a cycle,
  the cycle is never broken by drawing a shell before a part it encloses.
  Inverted ink hulls keep today's order. Every existing golden is
  byte-unchanged; the curl example changes only where a cycle used to put
  a bone over its skin (see the decision log).
- **AC2. The peel.** `Peel` is built from a lift (m), an axis, a span
  along it and a stagger in `[0, 1)`; bad input is a typed error naming
  the field. `Peel::apply(mesh, u)` gives each face its own vertices,
  moved by `lift · e` along the face's outward unit normal, and its
  material at `opacity · (1 − e)`, where `e` is the smoothstep of the
  face's own progress. A face whose centroid sits at the span's start
  starts at `u = 0`; one at its end starts at `u = stagger`; every face
  ends at `u = 1`. At `u = 0` the render is byte-identical to the unpeeled
  mesh; at `u = 1` every face is fully transparent. Faces stay valid.
- **AC3. Highlight.** `Material::mix(other, t)` blends the tone ramps and
  the opacity, `t` clamped to `[0, 1]`, exact at both ends, rounding each
  channel to the nearest integer. The layer and the seam are the first
  material's.
- **AC4. One rig for three layers.** The layers are separate OBJ files
  whose objects are named `segment.part`; every segment is one of the
  rig's (`joints.tsv`: segment, parent, joint), so one motor track per
  segment poses skin, muscle and bone together. A test checks every object
  names a known segment and every layer covers the segments it must.
- **AC5. The layers are the house toy.** Skin matches the curl example's
  toy proportions (capsule limbs, faceted head, headband, eyes); muscle
  has biceps, triceps, deltoid, lats, pecs, glutes, quads, hamstrings and
  calves (plus forearm flexors), each its own material; bone has a skull,
  a segmented spine, a ribcage hint, the pelvis, scapulae, clavicles,
  humerus, radius and ulna, femur, tibia and fibula, and block hands and
  feet. All low-poly (at most 4 000 triangles a layer, ink hulls
  included), generated by
  `tools/blender/anatomy.py` from fixed parameters and committed with it.
  No third-party model.
- **AC6. Determinism.** Two renders of a peel, and a render on four
  threads, are byte-identical. A trig-free golden PPM shows a skin cube
  half peeled over a muscle box over a bone octahedron.
- **AC7. The kernel stays thin.** Zero new dependencies; in the core.

## 4. Constraints & non-goals

- **Not soft-body or anatomical accuracy.** Muscles are convex faceted
  volumes; the reel's point is which group works, not its fibres.
- **Not skinning.** A part rides one segment's motor; nothing bends
  across a joint. The capsule limbs already hide the seam.
- **Not a peel of interpenetrating shells.** Enclosure is a convex test;
  parts that cross keep the mean-depth fallback (R-0018 §4).
- **Bands still follow the population.** `Faceted` quantises tones over
  the batch, so removing the peeled skin can move a tone by one band; the
  example removes it while the camera is moving.

| 2026-11-22 | **Accepted** and **In progress** on the owner's brief («eso elevaría el juego a nivel pro») | The coordinator's message relaying the owner, 2026-11-22 |
| 2026-11-22 | **An enclosure test before the mean-depth fallback** | Off-centre nesting (a biceps forward of the arm's axis) gives the inner part a nearer mean depth than the visible half of its shell, so the fallback drew the muscle over the skin. "Inside every face plane" is exact for a convex shell and still sound for any shell (it means inside the planes' intersection) |
| 2026-11-22 | **A cycle is broken at the farthest part that waits on no part it encloses** | With about 100 parts the mean-depth orders form cycles, and breaking one at the farthest part of all drew the torso's skin over its pecs in the anatomy example. Protecting every exact order (separating planes too) was tried and rejected: it changed most of the curl example's frames and drew ink hulls over neighbouring parts. Protecting enclosures alone fixes the anatomy and changes the curl example only in 48 of 150 frames, all but two in the x-ray, where a bone is now always under its skin's glass instead of over it in some frames (curl MP4 `c79eb3bc…` → `e696d2d3…`; gym `3050d919…` → `af4700b3…` the same way). The goldens of R-0018 and R-0019 are unchanged |
| 2026-11-22 | **Triangle budget 4 000 a layer, ink hulls included** | The skin is the curl toy's density (capsule limbs, 8 sides) with a hull per part: 3 416 triangles. Blender stores positions in `float32`, so tilted capsule quads are not planar to `PLANAR_TOL` and the loader splits them (R-0020 AC2); the count is of triangles either way |
| 2026-11-22 | **The peel is a mesh-to-mesh function, not a scene or object field** | `Object` and `Scene` are public-field structs callers build field by field (R-0018 OQ-3); a new mesh per frame costs nothing they need to know about |
| 2026-11-22 | **The stagger is by position along an axis, not by face rank** | A figure is many objects; a position keys every face of every part on one schedule, so the peel runs down the whole body, not part by part |
| 2026-11-22 | **Muscles are `Layer::Skin` by binding, not by name** | Under `XRay` the muscle must be glass, which is what `Skin` means to the finish; naming them `skin_*` would mislabel them. The example binds the layer |
