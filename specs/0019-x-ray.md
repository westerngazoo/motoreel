## 1. Motivation

The house's x-ray reveal as a finish, with the spike's stacking glow fixed.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 `XRay`

`XRay::new(solid: Faceted, glass: Glass { tint, alpha, edge_width,
edge_alpha }, glow: Glow { width, alpha }) -> Result<XRay, XRayError>`.

`paint` walks the batch in **runs** of consecutive surfaces with equal
material:

- `Solid`: each surface is `solid.prim(s, band)`, the band from
  `solid.bands_of(batch)`: exactly what `Faceted` paints (AC2).
- `Skin`: one `Prim2::Path` whose subpaths are the run's outlines, filled
  `tint` at `alpha`, stroked `tint` at `edge_width`/`edge_alpha` (AC3).
- `Inner`: one stroke-only `Prim2::Path` over the run's outlines in the
  material's lightest tone (`glow`), then each surface opaque at its band,
  banded among the batch's inner surfaces (AC4, AC5).

Why one primitive: both sinks composite a primitive's fill once per pixel
under the nonzero union and its stroke once per pixel (union by max), so
faces inside one primitive cannot stack. Front faces are all
counter-clockwise in the image, so their union never cancels.

### 2.2 `Sweep`

`Sweep::new(swept, unswept, at, toward)`; a point is swept when
`(p − at)·toward < 0`. `paint` runs **both** finishes on the whole batch
(tones stay put while the line moves), then clips every output primitive to
its half-plane: Sutherland–Hodgman against one line, exact for any
polygon; the crossing on edge `p → q` is computed from `p` in both halves,
so they share their cut points bit for bit. Swept output is emitted first;
the halves are disjoint, so depth order is kept. A subpath with a cubic
goes whole to the side of its start; a non-path primitive to the side of
its first point.
